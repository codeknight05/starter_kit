=== EXPERIMENT 1: split(' ') vs split() ===
Hypothesis: repeated spaces create empty word entries and deflate fertility.
Command: python your-submission/partA/audit_fertility.py
Input: corpus_sample/eng_sample.txt, line 7 and the full 10-line sample.
Target Line: 'Please keep the books  in the cupboard.'
  Tokens: 10
  Naive line.split(' '): 8 words -> Fertility = 1.2500
  Clean line.split():    7 words -> Fertility = 1.4286
  Absolute delta: +0.1786
  Relative distortion: 12.50% deflation
English Sample Corpus Overall:
  Naive sum(split(' ')): 79 words -> Fertility = 1.2532
  Clean sum(split()):    78 words -> Fertility = 1.2692

  Corpus-level relative distortion: 1.27% deflation
Interpretation: the implementation bug is real, but its measured corpus-level effect is small on this sample.

=== EXPERIMENT 2: Macro-average vs micro-average ===
Hypothesis: the two aggregation rules produce different corpus statistics when sentences have different word counts.
Command: python your-submission/partA/audit_fertility.py
Input: corpus_sample/* and partA/eval_corpus/*; GPT-2; clean split().
--- Sample Corpus ---
  [eng] Macro-Avg: 1.2831 | Micro-Avg: 1.2692 | Delta: +0.0138 (+1.09%)
  [hin] Macro-Avg: 7.5985 | Micro-Avg: 7.5246 | Delta: +0.0739 (+0.98%)

--- Eval Corpus (250 lines) ---
  [eng] Macro-Avg: 1.3350 | Micro-Avg: 1.3189 | Delta: +0.0161 (+1.22%)
  [hin] Macro-Avg: 7.8644 | Micro-Avg: 7.8250 | Delta: +0.0393 (+0.50%)
  [kan] Macro-Avg: 22.2279 | Micro-Avg: 22.0269 | Delta: +0.2010 (+0.91%)
  [tam] Macro-Avg: 24.6861 | Micro-Avg: 24.6026 | Delta: +0.0835 (+0.34%)

Interpretation: macro gives every sentence equal weight; micro gives aggregate tokens/words equal weight. Micro is the relevant choice for corpus-level token cost, but macro is not universally invalid.

=== EXPERIMENT 3: Whitespace words as a cross-language denominator ===
Hypothesis: whitespace-word counts do not hold comparable semantic payload constant across aligned languages.
Command: python your-submission/partA/audit_fertility.py
Input: 250 aligned FLORES rows; GPT-2; Kannada compared with English.
English Eval Set:   5366 total words (21.5 words/sent) | Tok/Word: 1.26
Kannada Eval Set:   4156 total words (16.6 words/sent) | Tok/Word: 22.02
Tok/Word Ratio (Kan/Eng): 17.52x worse
Tok/Sentence Ratio (Kan/Eng): 13.57x worse
Absolute ratio difference: +3.95x
Relative distortion: 29.1% higher by tok/word
Interpretation: parallel sentences approximately hold meaning constant, while whitespace words, graphemes, and bytes do not. Sentence tokens are a useful first-order aligned comparison, not a universal production denominator.

=== EXPERIMENT 4: Tokenizer vocabulary effect ===
Hypothesis: token count inflation depends strongly on tokenizer/model vocabulary, not only on script.
Command: python your-submission/partA/audit_fertility.py
Input: 250 aligned FLORES rows; GPT-2 versus XLM-R; grapheme denominator.
lang    GPT2 Tok/Graph   XLM-R Tok/Graph    XLM-R/GPT2    Reduction
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
eng              0.206             0.233          1.13x       -13.1%
hin              2.202             0.426          0.19x        80.6%
kan              4.052             0.464          0.11x        88.6%
tam              4.205             0.422          0.10x        90.0%

Interpretation: XLM-R produces fewer tokens per aligned sentence on this corpus, demonstrating tokenizer/model-vocabulary dependence. This does not by itself prove an equal end-to-end serving-cost or latency improvement.
